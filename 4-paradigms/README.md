# 🧩 Paradigms Experiment
## Five Ways of Thinking Solve One Problem

### 🎯 The Problem

**Medical diagnosis: Predict cardiac risk** given:
- Age
- Cholesterol level
- Blood pressure
- Exercise frequency

**One dataset. Five different thinking paradigms. Same goal.**

### 💭 The Five Paradigms

| # | Paradigm | Philosopher | Method | Strength | Weakness |
|---|----------|-------------|--------|----------|----------|
| 1 | **Rule-Based** | Parmenides | If-Then rules | Interpretable | Brittle |
| 2 | **Predicate Logic** | Aristotle | Formal logic + probability | Sound reasoning | Slow |
| 3 | **Neural Network** | Leibniz | Distributed representation | Learns patterns | Black box |
| 4 | **Bayesian** | Hume | Probability + priors | Principled uncertainty | Computationally hard |
| 5 | **k-Nearest Neighbor** | Epicurus | Empirical similarity | Stores experience | Needs all data at inference |

### 🏛 Philosophy

- **Parmenides** — Being is unchanging, predictable by simple rules
- **Aristotle** — Logical categories and syllogisms
- **Leibniz** — Monads (atoms) create the whole; neural networks echo this
- **Hume** — Knowledge from experience and probability
- **Epicurus** — Empirical observation and similarity

### 📊 Live Metrics

For each paradigm, see real-time:
- **Accuracy** — % correct predictions
- **Precision** — Of predicted positives, how many are actually positive?
- **Recall** — Of actual positives, how many did we find?
- **F1 Score** — Harmonic mean of precision and recall
- **Confusion Matrix** — True/False Positives/Negatives

### 🚀 How to Use

1. Open `index.html`
2. Train all five methods on the same data
3. Slide the **complexity** slider to see how performance changes
4. Hover over metrics for explanations
5. See the radar chart compare all five at once

### 📈 What to Expect

- **Rule-based** — Fast, high precision, low recall (too conservative)
- **Logic** — Balanced but slow
- **Neural** — Highest accuracy overall, especially with complex data
- **Bayesian** — Best uncertainty estimates
- **kNN** — Good recall, sometimes overfits

### 💡 The Insight

There's no "best" way to think. Each paradigm is:
- A different **lens** on the problem
- Strong in different **domains**
- Useful for different **questions**

Modern AI often **combines** them (e.g., Bayesian neural networks, ensemble methods).
